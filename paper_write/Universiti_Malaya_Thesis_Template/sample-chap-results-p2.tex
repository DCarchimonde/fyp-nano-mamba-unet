\chapter{DISCUSSION AND LIMITATION}
\label{chap:discussion}

This chapter provides a profound analytical interpretation of the objective empirical results presented in Chapter 6. Moving systematically beyond statistical reporting, this section mathematically interprets the ablation study outcomes through the lens of representation learning, discusses the anatomical intricacies influencing segmentation variance, and scientifically dissects the architectural limitations of the current study.

\section{Anatomical Discrepancies in Segmentation Efficacy}
A critical observation derived from the quantitative benchmarks is the consistent variance in segmentation accuracy across different cardiac structures. This phenomenon is not algorithmic, but fundamentally rooted in the inherent anatomical and physiological properties of the human heart \cite{bernard2018deep}. 

Across all evaluated models, the Left Ventricle (LV) consistently registers the highest DSC (e.g., 97.51\% for Nano-Mamba). This is mathematically predictable in image processing: the LV possesses a thick, well-defined myocardial wall and exhibits distinct contrast gradients against the blood pool in standard MRI physics. Conversely, the Myocardium (MYO) consistently exhibits the lowest accuracy (93.67\% for Nano-Mamba). The MYO segmentation is mathematically framed as a highly constrained topological problem; it is an annular (ring-like) structure encompassing the LV. The presence of papillary muscles—which share identical pixel intensity distributions with the myocardium but belong anatomically to the blood pool cavity—creates severe local minima in the optimization landscape, forcing all networks to struggle with precise boundary definition.

\section{Clinical Interpretation of Pathological Variance}
The empirical results cataloged in Chapter 6 explicitly demonstrate that the Nano-Mamba U-Net's segmentation fidelity is not uniformly distributed across all patient conditions. The marginal degradation observed in Myocardial Infarction (MINF) and Abnormal Right Ventricle (ARV) cases warrants a profound multidisciplinary interpretation, merging magnetic resonance physics with clinical pathology.

\subsection{Tissue Heterogeneity in Myocardial Infarction (MINF)}
The network recorded its lowest aggregate performance on the MINF cohort (95.22\% Mean DSC), specifically struggling with the Myocardium (92.73\%). This phenomenon is mathematically and biologically predictable. Myocardial Infarction results in localized ischemic necrosis (tissue death), which is subsequently replaced by fibrotic scar tissue \cite{petitjean2011review}. 

In standard SSFP cine-MRI sequences, this scar tissue drastically alters the native proton relaxation times ($T_1$ and $T_2$), causing localized hypointense (dark) or hyperintense (bright) focal artifacts within the normally homogeneous gray myocardial wall. Because convolutional neural networks inherently rely on textural homogeneity and spatial gradients to detect edges, the sudden appearance of a hyperintense scar can mathematically trick the local convolution filters into classifying the dead tissue as part of the bright blood pool. Despite the global contextual awareness provided by the Mamba bottleneck, the extreme local visual ambiguity of severe infarctions fundamentally caps the theoretical upper bound of pixel-wise accuracy, proving that pathological texture variation is a harder algorithmic challenge than pure geometric deformation.

\subsection{The Geometric Complexity of ARV and DCM}
Conversely, the primary challenge in Abnormal Right Ventricle (ARV) and Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) cases is topological rather than textural. ARV patients typically present with severe dilation or structural dysplasias of the right ventricle, causing the DSC for the RV to drop from a baseline of 97.45\% (Normal) to 94.88\% (ARV). 

The right ventricle natively possesses a complex, asymmetric crescent-like topology that wraps around the thicker left ventricle. In ARV conditions, this shape becomes wildly erratic, often featuring pronounced trabeculations (muscular ridges) that extend deep into the blood pool. Similarly, in DCM, the extreme expansion of the ventricular cavities stretches the myocardial wall to abnormal thinness. In both scenarios, the target structures often shrink to a thickness of merely 1 to 2 voxels at certain spatial coordinates. When the spatial thickness approaches the Nyquist–Shannon sampling limit of the MRI physical resolution, the Partial Volume Effect (PVE) effectively destroys the boundary gradient. The Nano-Mamba architecture's robust performance (still exceeding 95\% DSC on average) on these extreme geometric outliers unequivocally validates the efficacy of the continuous-time sequence modeling ($\mathbf{\bar{A}}h_{k-1} + \mathbf{\bar{B}}x_k$), which successfully forces the network to maintain topological continuity even when local spatial convolutions fail.

\section{The Overfitting Paradox of Massive Architectures}
A highly counterintuitive result derived from the benchmark is the sub-optimal performance of the SOTA SegResNet (93.94\% Mean DSC). Operating with an immense 18.80 million parameters, it is theoretically the most expressive model evaluated. However, this massive parameter space fundamentally violates the principle of parsimony (Occam's Razor) when applied to the constrained ACDC dataset. 

Given that the training cohort is restricted to 100 patients, the 18.80M parameters lead to severe spatial overfitting. The network memorizes the specific morphological idiosyncrasies of the training subjects rather than learning abstract, topology-invariant representations. This mathematically validates the necessity for ultra-lightweight architectures. The Nano-Mamba (1.45M parameters) implicitly regularizes the learning process by heavily constraining the hypothesis space, thereby achieving a superior 95.83\% DSC while avoiding the generalization gap observed in SegResNet. Furthermore, as depicted in the loss landscape curves (Figure \ref{fig:loss_curve}), the validation loss perfectly asymptotes alongside the training loss, further corroborating the absence of parameter-induced overfitting.

\section{Ablation Analysis and the Information Bottleneck}
The ablation studies deeply validate the structural necessity of the continuous-time State Space mechanism. When the Mamba module is entirely excised and replaced with standard dense convolutions (Ablation No Mamba), the Mean DSC significantly degrades to 93.73\%. This proves that relying strictly on $3 \times 3 \times 3$ localized volumetric kernels fails to capture the continuous spatio-temporal physiological motion of the cardiac cycle. 

More crucially, over-compressing the Mamba bottleneck to 64 channels (Half Mamba) leads to a catastrophic loss of spatio-temporal representation, plummeting the DSC to 80.40\% while inadvertently increasing the parameter count to 1.64M due to required dimensional adapters. This phenomenon is explicitly explained through the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle in deep learning \cite{tishby2015deep}. Restricting the channel capacity to 64 excessively constricts the latent space, irreversibly destroying the high-frequency spatial boundary information during the tensor serialization process. This mathematically confirms that the 128-channel Mamba configuration is the absolute optimal bound for preserving 4D cardiac dynamics.

\section{Failure Case Analysis and Architectural Limitations}
While the overall quantitative metrics are exceptionally high, scientific transparency mandates an explicit analysis of the model's limitations. Deterministic failure modes exist within the Nano-Mamba framework, predominantly clustered in the extreme apical (bottom) and basal (top) slices of the cardiac volume \cite{zotti2018gridnet}.

\subsection{The Apical Partial Volume Effect (PVE)}
The most prominent failure cases occur at the extreme apical slices of the Left Ventricle. At the apex, the ventricular cavity tapers into a minuscule volume. Due to the inherent thickness of the MRI slices (e.g., 5mm to 8mm) and low out-of-plane resolution, the Partial Volume Effect (PVE) becomes overwhelmingly dominant. PVE occurs when a single macroscopic voxel encompasses multiple distinct tissue types (e.g., myocardium and surrounding visceral fat), resulting in an averaged, blurred intensity value that strictly defies binary classification. In these specific edge-cases, the Nano-Mamba model occasionally exhibits subtle topological hallucinations, predicting disconnected fragments. Addressing these localized failures mathematically mandates the future integration of explicit 3D shape-prior regularizations.

\subsection{Cohort Size and Temporal Clinical Validation Limits}
Beyond algorithmic constraints, the empirical validation of this study was strictly bounded by the ACDC dataset, utilizing uniform Siemens scanners from a single geographic demographic. The model's cross-vendor robustness (e.g., performance on Philips or GE scanners) remains an unverified limitation. Furthermore, while the architecture segments static 3D volumes efficiently, it lacks longitudinal temporal validation to track disease progression across months or years for a single patient, which is essential for comprehensive chronic heart failure management.
