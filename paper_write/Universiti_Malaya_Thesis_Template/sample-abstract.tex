Cardiovascular diseases require precise segmentation of cardiac structures—namely the right ventricle, myocardium, and left ventricle—from MRI sequences for accurate diagnosis. While 3D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) like U-Net are the foundational standard, they lack inherent spatio-temporal modeling capabilities to understand the continuous dynamics of a beating heart. Conversely, modern Vision Transformers capture global context but suffer from quadratic computational complexity, heavily restricting their deployment on resource-constrained clinical edge devices. To address this paradigm, this research proposes the Nano-Mamba U-Net, an ultra-lightweight architecture integrating a Selective State Space Model (Mamba) into the U-Net bottleneck. This novel design allows for the linear-time sequential modeling of cardiac motion without the massive parameter overhead typical of self-attention mechanisms. Evaluated on the Automated Cardiac Diagnosis Challenge (ACDC) dataset, the proposed model achieves a highly competitive Mean Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) of 95.83\%. Crucially, the Nano-Mamba U-Net requires only 1.45 million parameters—a staggering 92.2\% reduction compared to the state-of-the-art SegResNet (18.80 million)—while maintaining a real-time inference speed of 12.26 Frames Per Second (FPS) on a consumer-grade RTX 4060 GPU. Ablation studies further confirm that the 128-channel Mamba bottleneck is mathematically optimal for balancing parameter efficiency and spatial boundary fidelity. Ultimately, this research demonstrates that integrating State Space Models into medical image segmentation yields a highly efficient, spatio-temporally aware framework, effectively paving the way for advanced cardiac diagnostic AI on portable clinical hardware.
