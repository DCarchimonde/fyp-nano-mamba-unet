\chapter{CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK}
\label{chap:conclusion}

The final chapter brings together the experimental findings, answers each research question, and sets out the next steps. The conclusion is based on both the revised patient-level segmentation experiment and the complete-cine post-hoc analysis reported in Chapter 6, together with the interpretation in Chapter 7. The purpose is not to overstate the proposed model, but to identify precisely what was achieved, what was not achieved, and what remains open.

\section{Restatement of the Research Aim}
The aim of this thesis was to design and evaluate a lightweight Mamba-inspired U-Net architecture for cardiac MRI segmentation and to determine how its segmentations can support a bounded full-cine cardiac function and motion analysis. The motivation came from a practical research problem: cardiac segmentation models should not only achieve high Dice scores, but should also be computationally manageable, reproducibly evaluated, and connected transparently to downstream cardiac-cycle measurements. Nano-Mamba U-Net integrates a sequence-processing bottleneck into a 3D U-Net-style encoder-decoder network. Compressed volumetric features are serialized into a one-dimensional spatial sequence, processed by a lightweight gated module inspired by Mamba, and reshaped back into the 3D feature space.

Nano-Mamba U-Net is not presented as a complete implementation of the original Mamba selective scan. Nor do the experiments establish official ACDC test-set performance, dense tissue motion, regional strain, or readiness for clinical use. The demonstrated contribution is narrower: a compact Mamba-inspired bottleneck is evaluated for its accuracy--efficiency balance, and the chosen spatial checkpoint is subsequently used to obtain traceable functional and global-motion trajectories over every validation cine phase.

\section{Summary of Research Contributions}
The work produced the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Implemented architecture:} The resulting Nano-Mamba U-Net performs 3D segmentation of RV, MYO, and LV. It retains a compact encoder-decoder and inserts a Mamba-inspired operation in the compressed bottleneck. The novelty lies in integrating and testing that design, not in inventing either U-Net or Mamba.
    \item \textbf{Patient-separated evaluation:} Leakage was controlled with a deterministic 80/20 split at patient level. From the ACDC training cohort, 160 cases entered training and 40 held-out cases entered validation. This design replaces the looser validation used by the earlier exploratory scripts.
    \item \textbf{Measured accuracy--efficiency balance:} With 1.46M parameters, Nano-Mamba U-Net reached 84.78\% mean DSC and a post-hoc patient-bootstrap 95\% interval of 82.05--86.90\%. It exceeded the 3D U-Net baseline while using fewer parameters, but SegResNet16 remained the accuracy leader at 86.70\% mean DSC.
    \item \textbf{Ablation evidence:} The zero-Mamba convolutional control reached 85.64\% mean DSC and therefore finished slightly above Nano. In return, Nano required 36.3\% fewer parameters, retained approximately 99.0\% of the control's Dice, and had an effectively unchanged recorded FPS. The evidence is therefore a lightweight trade-off rather than proof that the gate improves accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Analysis of complete cines:} The selected checkpoint processed 550 phases belonging to 20 validation patients. Native-grid masks were used to trace LV/RV/MYO volumes and derive ED/ES timing and EF. A global myocardial radial-distance surrogate and LV-centroid displacement supplied two motion summaries. Frame-level and patient-level rows were retained so that these outputs could be audited.
    \item \textbf{Finding for fixed temporal fusion:} Circular adjacent-frame probability averaging did not resolve an improvement in endpoint Dice or EF. It did, however, lower normalized LV-curve second difference in 19 of 20 patients, with the paired 95\% bootstrap interval entirely below zero. This supports smoother curves, not learned motion.
    \item \textbf{Bounded scientific claims:} The final account excludes unsupported assertions of absolute state-of-the-art performance, full Mamba implementation, learned temporal behaviour, dense cardiac motion tracking, and clinical readiness. Positive and negative experimental evidence are both retained in the interpretation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Answers to Research Questions}
\textbf{Research Question 1: How can compressed 3D cardiac MRI feature maps be serialized and processed by a Mamba-inspired bottleneck within a U-Net-style segmentation architecture?}

The project answers this question through the Nano-Mamba U-Net design. A 3D encoder first compresses the MRI volume into a lower-resolution latent feature map. This tensor is reshaped from $B \times C \times H \times W \times D$ into a one-dimensional sequence of $HWD$ tokens, processed by a lightweight Mamba-inspired gated block, and then reshaped back into volumetric form before the decoder reconstructs the segmentation mask. The executed block uses kernel-3 depthwise mixing and a token-wise scalar gate; it has no selective scan, recurrent state propagation, attention, or direct global all-token interaction. This demonstrates a practical compact sequence-inspired transformation, not a full Mamba/SSM implementation.

The significance of this design is that the gated processing is applied at the bottleneck, where the spatial resolution is already reduced. This keeps the computational cost manageable. If the same operation were applied directly to the full-resolution input volume, the token length would be much larger and the memory burden would increase substantially. Therefore, the bottleneck placement reflects an engineering compromise between local serialized mixing and computational feasibility.

However, this design also has a limitation. Because the bottleneck operates after several down-sampling steps, some fine boundary information may already have been compressed by the encoder. The sequence module can mix the remaining latent features, but it cannot fully recover details that were lost earlier. This trade-off helps account for a model that is compact and competitive, yet not top-ranked in the final validation experiment.

\textbf{Research Question 2 (restated): How does the held-out validation Dice of Nano-Mamba U-Net compare with 3D U-Net, Attention U-Net, SegResNet, and the ablation models?}

Only in part. Nano-Mamba U-Net achieved 84.78\% mean DSC, outperforming the 3D U-Net baseline (80.83\%) and Attention U-Net (74.78\%). The patient-paired post-hoc intervals for both of those differences remained above zero. In contrast, Nano-Mamba scored below the 86.70\% obtained by SegResNet16 and the 85.64\% obtained by the zero-Mamba convolutional control; both paired intervals were below zero. The 64-channel Mamba-bottleneck ablation reached 84.95\%, and its paired interval against Nano crossed zero. These comparisons place Nano-Mamba among the competitive models, but not at the top of the Dice ranking, and they do not amount to statistical validation on an independent cohort.

The comparison is informative precisely because it does not yield one simple winner. Looking only at 3D U-Net would favour Nano-Mamba, whereas looking only at SegResNet16 would suggest the opposite. All models must be considered together: the proposed network improves on some baselines, remains close to an ablation, and trails the strongest residual segmentation model.

The final answer to this research question is therefore conditional: Nano-Mamba U-Net is competitive in held-out validation performance, but it is not the strongest model by absolute Dice. This conclusion is more cautious than the original thesis draft, but it is more defensible for academic assessment.

\textbf{Research Question 3: What accuracy-efficiency trade-off is observed when comparing Nano-Mamba U-Net with heavier or non-Mamba alternatives?}

Nano-Mamba U-Net achieved the smallest reported parameter count among all evaluated models, with 1.46M parameters. Using the unrounded CSV values, it used approximately 69.7\% fewer reported parameters than the 3D U-Net baseline, approximately 69.0\% fewer than SegResNet16, 36.3\% fewer than the zero-Mamba convolutional control, and 11.1\% fewer than the 64-channel Mamba-bottleneck ablation. Against the latter ablation, Nano retained 99.8\% of mean DSC and was only 0.174 percentage points lower; the paired interval crossed zero, so no reliable accuracy penalty was established, although formal equivalence was not tested. The reported parameter count includes unused legacy gate-projection outputs, so it describes serialized trainable tensors rather than effective parameters only. The trade-off is that this compactness came with lower Dice than SegResNet16 and the convolutional control. The final interpretation is that Nano-Mamba U-Net offers a lightweight design, but not the highest absolute segmentation accuracy.

This trade-off is central to the thesis. A single Dice ranking does not describe the full engineering result. Fewer parameters matter only if the resulting masks remain sufficiently accurate, while an accurate but much larger network may not suit every hardware setting. Nano-Mamba occupies that middle position in the present comparison: it is the smallest evaluated model and exceeds the basic 3D U-Net baseline, although SegResNet16 still gives the highest Dice.

\textbf{Research Question 4: How can the selected spatial segmentation checkpoint support full-cine cardiac function and global motion analysis, and what measurable effect does fixed adjacent-frame probability fusion have relative to frame-wise inference?}

The selected epoch-121 checkpoint was applied independently to all 550 3D phases from the 20 validation cines. Each frame followed the checkpoint-compatible resize and normalization path. Predicted categorical masks were then restored to the native spatial array shape. Header voxel sizes converted class counts and centroids into millilitres and millimetres. LV-volume trajectories supported ED/ES detection, EDV, ESV, stroke volume, and EF; successive masks also supported global centroid-displacement and myocardial radial-distance trajectories. Endpoint image identity, historical Dice reproduction, manual-mask endpoint Dice, EF, phase timing, artifact hashes, and all aggregate arithmetic were audited.

Relative to frame-wise inference, temporal fusion changed resized-grid endpoint Dice from 84.779\% to 84.794\% and EF MAE from 3.514 to 3.329 percentage points. Both paired bootstrap intervals included zero, so neither change is a resolved improvement. The normalized LV-curve second difference fell from 0.03252 to 0.03139; the paired interval $[-0.00195,-0.00051]$ excluded zero and 19 of 20 patient curves were smoother. Thus, the supported temporal contribution is smoother segmentation-derived global trajectories from a fixed post-hoc filter. It is not evidence that the network learned cardiac time or tissue motion.

\section{Implications of the Findings}
The findings have four main implications. First, U-Net-style architectures remain strong foundations for cardiac MRI segmentation. The success of SegResNet16 and the zero-Mamba convolutional control shows that convolutional and residual components are still highly effective on this dataset. This prevents the thesis from making an exaggerated claim that new sequence-inspired modules automatically outperform established convolutional methods.

Second, the experiment leaves a reason to investigate small sequence-inspired modules further. Nano-Mamba produced a competitive validation score while using the fewest parameters. In this case, inserting compact gated sequence processing at the bottleneck did not cause a major loss of segmentation performance. That observation motivates further experiments, but it does not show that the module is superior.

Third, the validation design changes how much confidence can be placed in the comparison. Scores fell relative to the earlier exploratory evaluation after patients were separated between partitions. The lower estimates are more credible because frames from the same person no longer cross the split. For medical AI, that protection against leakage is more informative than retaining an inflated Dice value from a weaker protocol.

Fourth, complete-cycle measurements can be derived from a spatial segmenter without claiming dense tracking. Processing all cine frames yielded trajectories for volume, phase, EF, centroid position, and radial distance, and fixed fusion made the volume curve less rough. The Dice and EF differences were unresolved, however, and intermediate phases have no manual masks. Each downstream quantity must therefore be interpreted within its own validation limits.

\section{Future Work}
Several extensions follow directly from the limitations of the present experiment.

\subsection{Evaluation on External and Official Test Sets}
The most direct next test is evaluation on either the official ACDC test set or a separate external cardiac MRI cohort. This would measure generalisation beyond the internal patient split. Scanner vendor, acquisition protocol, spatial resolution, and the patient population can all change medical images, so performance on the current cohort cannot establish performance under those conditions.

\subsection{Larger Pathology-Specific Validation}
The completed cine analysis uses actual ACDC metadata for its descriptive diagnostic-group summaries. Within the validation cohort, however, HCM is represented by one patient and RV abnormality by two. Pathology-specific conclusions require larger, externally sampled groups and subgroup analyses defined in advance. A study of that size could examine failures of segmentation and EF estimation without over-interpreting very small internal groups.

\subsection{Additional Boundary-Based Metrics}
Dice Similarity Coefficient is the principal overlap measure in the present thesis, but it does not fully characterize boundary quality. Future work should therefore add boundary measures, for example Average Surface Distance or Hausdorff Distance. This is especially important for myocardium, where a small boundary error may affect a clinical measurement even when the overlap score appears acceptable.

\subsection{Comparison with Full Mamba/SSM Implementations}
The implemented module is deliberately Mamba-inspired and does not contain the complete selective scan. A comparison with an official or optimized Mamba implementation could test whether a more faithful state space module improves cardiac segmentation. Comparing the two would show whether the observed limitation belongs to this simplified bottleneck or to sequence modeling of compressed cardiac features more generally.

\subsection{Multi-Scale Sequence Modeling}
Only the bottleneck contains the Mamba-inspired block in the current model. A later architecture could evaluate clearly specified sequence or state-space operations at several encoder or decoder scales. Earlier access to higher-resolution features might retain more boundary detail and permit different latent interactions. It would also increase memory demand and implementation complexity, so any benefit must be measured rather than assumed.

\subsection{Learned Temporal and Dense Motion Modeling}
All phases of each 4D cine are processed in this study, but the trained model still receives them one at a time and uses fixed fusion afterwards. A future network could accept several phases jointly, with temporal features trained under an explicit consistency objective. Such a model should be compared against the current frame-wise and fixed-fusion baselines, rather than assuming that a learned temporal block is automatically better.

Dense motion modeling is a separate extension. Optical flow or deformable registration could establish spatial correspondence between myocardial points, enabling regional displacement and strain rather than only global centroid and radial-distance surrogates. This would require suitable ground truth or independently validated clinical measurements, topology checks, and evaluation beyond ED/ES overlap.

\section{Concluding Remarks}
This thesis demonstrates that a compact Mamba-inspired bottleneck can be integrated into a 3D U-Net-style architecture for cardiac MRI segmentation. The final segmentation experiment does not show absolute state-of-the-art accuracy, but it does show competitive internal-validation performance with the smallest parameter count among the evaluated models. The complete-cine extension further demonstrates how one frozen spatial checkpoint can generate auditable cardiac-cycle volume, function, phase, and global motion trajectories under practical hardware constraints.

The most important conclusion is that the proposed model should be understood as an accuracy--efficiency trade-off model embedded in a completed but bounded spatio-temporal workflow. It is not a universal replacement for stronger residual segmentation networks and not a complete clinical motion-analysis system. The evidence supports the internal six-model comparison, full-cine segmentation-derived functional analysis, and a curve-smoothness benefit from fixed temporal fusion. Independent testing, multi-seed and matched-capacity training, native-space model training, boundary metrics, larger subgroup evaluation, learned temporal representations, and dense motion or strain validation would extend this work into a stronger cardiac image-analysis framework.
